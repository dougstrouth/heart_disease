About Dataset
Context

This is a multivariate type of dataset which means providing or involving a variety of separate mathematical or statistical variables, multivariate numerical data analysis. It is composed of 14 attributes which are age, sex, chest pain type, resting blood pressure, serum cholesterol, fasting blood sugar, resting electrocardiographic results, maximum heart rate achieved, exercise-induced angina, oldpeak — ST depression induced by exercise relative to rest, the slope of the peak exercise ST segment, number of major vessels and Thalassemia. This database includes 76 attributes, but all published studies relate to the use of a subset of 14 of them. The Cleveland database is the only one used by ML researchers to date. One of the major tasks on this dataset is to predict based on the given attributes of a patient that whether that particular person has heart disease or not and other is the experimental task to diagnose and find out various insights from this dataset which could help in understanding the problem more.

About Dataset
Heart Disease Dataset Documentation

This synthetic dataset contains clinical and lifestyle attributes for individuals, with a binary target indicating the presence of heart disease. Each row represents one individual (patient record). The data appear numeric and categorical-encoded, suitable for classification tasks such as predicting heart disease risk.
High-level Overview

    Rows (records): Each record corresponds to a single patient.
    Columns (features): Mix of demographic, clinical, test results, and lifestyle indicators.
    Target: heart_disease (0 = no heart disease, 1 = heart disease).
    Typical uses:
        Supervised learning for risk prediction (classification).
        Exploratory data analysis of cardiovascular risk factors.
        Feature importance and model interpretability research.

Notes on Encodings and Ranges

    Several clinical fields (cp, restecg, slope, ca, thal, plus binary flags like fbs, exang, smoking, diabetes) are encoded as integers; treat them as categorical unless domain recoding is applied.
    Typical physiological ranges:
        trestbps: adult resting BP, generally 90–200+ mm Hg.
        chol: typical adult cholesterol 100–600 mg/dL.
        thalach: usually between 60–210 bpm depending on age/fitness.
        oldpeak: non-negative, commonly 0–6 (ST segment depression).
        bmi: usually 15–45 kg/m² in clinical datasets but may include wider values.
    ca typically ranges 0–3; values outside this may indicate alternative encoding.

Data Quality Considerations

    Check for outliers in clinical measures (chol, trestbps, oldpeak, bmi) that might require winsorization or domain review.
    Validate categorical codebooks (e.g., thal, cp, restecg, slope) to ensure correct mapping to clinical meanings.
    Assess class balance of heart_disease for model selection and evaluation strategy.
    Verify missingness; encoded zeros may represent valid negatives or missing surrogates depending on source conventions.

Recommended Preprocessing

    Cast categorical integers to categorical data types with meaningful labels.
    Scale/normalize continuous variables for distance-based or regularized models.
    Consider domain-specific feature engineering:
        Derive blood pressure categories.
        Age groups or interaction terms (e.g., age × thalach).
        Risk composite scores if clinically justified.

Example Use Cases

    Train/test split with stratification on heart_disease.
    Baselines: logistic regression, tree-based models (RF/GBM), and calibration assessment.
    Model explainability: SHAP for global and local factor contributions.
